## Detention is a process, not a complaint

Detention recovery fails when it starts as anger at hour five. It succeeds when it starts as documentation at hour one: arrival time, check-in, door assignment (or lack of one), and who was notified.

## Operating steps

1. **Confirm free time** on the rate confirmation before the truck arrives.
2. **Driver check-in evidence** — time-stamped notes, gate photos if appropriate, GPS breadcrumb.
3. **Notify the broker early** — at or before free time expires, with facts only.
4. **Update on a schedule** — every hour or per the broker’s process; do not vanish.
5. **Capture paperwork** — detention approval emails/texts, BOL notes, and tracker entries.
6. **Hand to billing cleanly** — so collections is not reconstructing history from chat.

## Tone that keeps the next load possible

Brokers get defensive when they feel ambushed. Lead with timestamps and a clear ask: “Truck checked in at 09:12, free time ended 11:12, still no door as of 12:15 — please confirm detention approval and an updated loading estimate.” That is professional pressure, not a fight.

## When not to die on the hill

Sometimes the relationship or the next freight matters more than squeezing every dollar of a disputed hour — especially with a strategic broker lane. Make that a conscious trade, logged for the manager, not an accidental giveaway by a tired dispatcher.

## Takeaway

Accessorial discipline protects margin and signals that your desk runs a real operation. Sloppy detention handling teaches brokers they can ignore you.
