## The coverage model

US Pacific business hours can be covered from India with a deliberate overnight/early shift design. The point is not “someone is awake.” The point is that **decisions and communication quality** match what a domestic desk would deliver at 09:00 in Covington or Kent.

## What must be true

- Shared tracker as source of truth (loads, ETAs, open risks)
- Named escalation owner for driver emergencies and broker fires
- Overlap windows with US-based leadership or customers when needed
- Written SOPs so quality does not depend on one hero dispatcher
- Clear rules for what can be decided overnight vs. what waits for a US callback

## Benefits for US carriers and brokers

- Continuity across night moves and early pickups
- Morning boards that are already groomed
- Faster response on after-hours exceptions
- Cost-efficient senior dispatch talent without giving up English-language professionalism

## Risks to manage (honestly)

- Cultural and holiday calendar mismatches — plan coverage
- Power/internet redundancy for remote staff
- Ambiguity in authority (“can I re-power without asking?”)
- Fatigue if shifts are understaffed

These are solvable with staffing, SOPs, and management attention — not reasons to dismiss the model.

## What to tell recruiters

Harinder’s operating pattern is remote US Pacific coverage from Punjab with hands-on dry-van experience, desk leadership, and comfort spanning broker negotiation through compliance-aware planning. For India hiring, the same skill set maps to logistics control towers, fleet ops, and process-heavy coordination roles in Mohali/Chandigarh and broader India logistics hubs.

## Takeaway

Timezone is a design problem. Solved well, India overnight coverage becomes a reliability feature — not a compromise.
