## The constraint

When a fleet moves to a new MC authority, brokers and load boards do not pause for your paperwork. Coverage downtime is optional — if the cutover is planned like a dispatch problem, not only a compliance problem.

## Workstreams that must run in parallel

1. **Equipment & insurance** — certificates, truck/trailer IDs, ELD/GPS continuity.
2. **Broker onboarding** — packets, W-9/authority docs, portal setups, factoring continuity if applicable.
3. **Load continuity** — which loads stay on old authority through delivery vs. which book on new.
4. **Driver communication** — what changes for them day-of (BOLs, signage instructions, who to call).
5. **Desk scripts** — how dispatch answers “are you under a new MC?” without creating doubt.

## Eight-day style execution principles

- Freeze non-critical experiments; run a known lane set.
- Assign a single cutover owner on the desk.
- Maintain a living checklist visible to leadership.
- Pre-warn strategic brokers with a professional notice and go-live timing.
- Keep at least one “escape” truck plan if a portal approval lags.

## Success definition

Zero uncovered loads, brokers still calling, drivers clear on instructions, and compliance packets complete enough that sales is not inventing answers.

## Takeaway

Authority transitions are operations projects. Treat them like one and the fleet keeps earning while the legal entity catches up.
